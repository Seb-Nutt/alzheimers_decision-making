The standard route that a participant expiriances through this simulation is as follows. The first 10 rounds is often the period with the biggest losses as the participants take larger risks as they choose cards A & B, looking for a quick boost early on as they prioritize short-term reward. After this point, the participant begins to stick with the safer options, resulting in an overall profit due the long term investment strategies employed. However, in participants with Alzhiemer's disease, the long-term risk weighing strategies and decisions that are normally used by the control group are not considered due to the damage to essential brain compenents involved in planning and executing these long-term strategies. This results in much riskier play, as the brain begins to only look at short-term payoff which the cards A & B provide. This leads to long-term losses which become increasingly dire through the simulation as the negative balance begins to stack higher and higher, eventually resulting in a negative final balance due to the lack of adaptability and learning when compared to the control group.
