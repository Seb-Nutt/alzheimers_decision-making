The brain stem is often the first region of the brain affected by Alzheimer's Disease. The brain stem processes and sends out information that is essential for the human body to function. Functions such as the heart beating, regulated breathing, and digestion are all controlled by the brains stem so the possible decay that Alzheimer’s presents poses a severe risk to the life of those affected by the disease. The cell necrosis within the brainstem causes it to suffer irreparable damage and, in later stages of the disease, affect the automatic essential functions provided by the brain stem which can cause irregular breathing, trouble swallowing and digesting, and erratic heart rates.
The disruptions within brain function that Alzheimer’s pushes on its patients has a large effect on the cerebellum. As the central hub for balance, motor learning, and voluntary muscle action, damage to the cerebellum will impact the motor systems that the body uses for every day activity. The high concentration of neurons in the cerebellum makes it very susceptible to the constant cell death from Alzheimer’s disease. The ability to create smooth motions, thoughts, and emotions will be disrupted as it kills the neurons that regulate those functions.
